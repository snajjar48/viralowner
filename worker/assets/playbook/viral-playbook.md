# Workplace Skit Edit Playbook

## 0. Pick the mode
- **Text-led silent** (~1/3 of videos, 0 words): premise overlay, one music/trending sound, visual payoff. Runs 5.8–18s with 1–7 shots.
- **Dialogue POV**: speech from frame 0, original room audio, no music.
- **Length**: median 9.5s (p25 6.4, p75 14.6). Keep single gags at 7s or less. Only three-step escalations earn 15–48s.

## 1. Hook (0–2s)
- First word at 0.00s (median). If the footage opens with a greeting or lead-in, trim into mid-sentence ('...work for Sammy') rather than open on silence.
- The only allowed silent open is about 0.6s of deadpan stare while the overlay is read.
- Wordless clips: the sound hits by 0.07–0.3s and the premise text is on frame 0.
- Start mid-action (pouring, scrubbing, scoop already in the tub), never on an establishing beat.
- State the stakes or a number within 1.5s ('$8', '20 minutes ago', 'closed in two minutes').
- The prop that becomes the punchline is visible in frame 1 (the crepe, the pile of spoons).
- Optional jolt cut at 0.3–0.73s.

## 2. Structure and beats
- Load the premise in the first 30–50%, and always by 10s.
- **Rule of three**: two expected beats, then a break ($20,000 → $15,000 → $0; manager → social media manager → the worker; fail, fail, succeed).
  - Each step runs longer than the last (sip about 2s → 4s → 6s).
- **Place the punchline by type**:
  - Spoken line or instant action: 85–97% (throw 92–96%, '$9' 93%, 'It's $8' 88%).
  - Visual reveal that keeps escalating: 40–60% (toilet 43%, water dump 53%, '$0' 52%). Spend the rest on hold, reaction and tag.
- The straight man agrees flatly ('Sure, no problem') within 0.24–0.5s, then the absurd action follows.
- Put the reaction before the reveal: a 1–1.5s confused or deadpan stare, then cut to the thing.
- Plant the target early and show it late (name at 0.54s, person at 4.75s). Keep the eventual punchline-giver visible in the background of setup shots.

## 3. Comedic timing
- Keep silence only when something visible happens in it. Median longest pause is 1.22s (p75 3.08s).
  - Before a reveal: 1–3s (drawer counting 3.08s, powdering a hand 1.46s).
  - When the action is the joke (eating, drinking): keep 3–10s uncut.
- Pauses grow as stakes rise (0.4 → 1s; 0.72 → 1.38s).
- Silent reaction cutaways run 1.3–1.4s, and the second one runs longer (~1.8s).
- Before the payoff cut, hold a deadpan look into the lens for 1–1.5s, then cut immediately.
- Cut to the reactor's face 0.3–0.5s before their line so the face reads first.
- Cut into the payoff action 0.1–0.2s before the setup line's last word ends.
- Rewards and answers come within 0.15s. Punishments come after about 1s.
- If the punchline image has detail to read, hold it about 2.5s (the toilet reveal holds 2.6s).

## 4. Dialogue editing
- Median gap between lines is 0.0s. Setup gaps stay at 0.45s or less.
- When a line trails off ('can I have a...'), butt the next line in with zero gap.
- Keep 'uh/um' only right before a reveal. Cut it from the setup.
- Never cut a word in half. The one exception is a deliberate cut-off in a 'time ran out' joke.
- Repeated prompts must be word-for-word identical.
- Nothing is spoken after the punchline except a short polite exit tag.

## 5. Pacing
- Medians: 3 shots, 2.93 cuts/10s, 2.13s per shot.
- 7 of 37 videos are one uncut shot. Use one take when the clip is under 8s and the gag is one action or one facial arc. Refresh the frame with performer movement, not cuts.
- Deliver information in 0.4–1.5s shots. Let the joke play in long takes (5–26s).
- Accelerate into the climax (0.7–1.3s shots), then settle on the final face.
- **Repeated-beat montages**: about 1.7s per shot. Action, then eye contact, then cut 0.3–0.7s later. Make the last shot the longest (~2s).
- Hard cuts only. None of these videos used speed ramps, digital zooms or transitions.
- Every cut must change the subject, angle or location.

## 6. Captions (speech)
- **When**: caption speech when the words carry the joke.
- **Style**: bold condensed ALL CAPS in white with a dark shadow, 2–4 words at a time, at 70–83% of frame height. The active word sits in a rounded pink/red box, synced word by word.
- Write money as '$7' / '$9' and highlight the number. Caption cut-off words ('GE-', 'HUR-') and groans ('UHHHH').
- Drop captions entirely during silent action.

## 7. On-screen text (premise overlay)
- **Formats**: 'pov: ...', 'When ...', 'my boss: ... / me:', 'Name:', or a quoted line in curly quotes.
- **Behavior**: on screen from frame 0 to the end, unchanged and unanimated.
- **Style**: lowercase or sentence case, white regular/medium sans, thin outline or soft shadow, no box. 2–3 centered lines (4 max).
- **Position**: 55–70% down by default; 17–25% from the top when the action sits low. Never cover the reacting face.
- Give the twist detail its own last line ('(we close at 11pm)'). Capitalize one stress word ('ARE').
- Label swaps land exactly on the cut ('me:' arrives with the reveal).
- Pin price tags on the object itself, visible only in that object's shot.
- When speech captions are also on screen, put the premise at 55–62% so it sits above them.
- **Exposé reveal**: switch to a B&W grade and stack 3 plain sentence-case lines, each escalating, 0.7–1.7s apart.

## 8. Audio and sound effects
- **Dialogue skits**: room tone and voices only. The silence is the payoff.
- **Wordless skits**: one sound running from frame 0.
  - Snap cuts within ±0.1s of beat hits (0.25s max).
  - The reveal cut lands 0–0.25s after the strongest hit.
  - Line the turn up with a tempo change or drop (0.55s → 0.185s hit spacing; 0.4s → 0.25s).
- **Borrowed speech audio**: keep its pauses intact and fill them with the visual payoff.
- **No added SFX**: virtually none were used. Impacts come from diegetic sound (spray, slurp) or the beat.
- **Music-led edits only**: a 0.5–1s black frame can serve as the pre-payoff pause.

## 9. Camera and framing choices
- **Service skits**: handheld customer POV across the counter at chest/eye height, with the register in the foreground. The customer is only a voice and hands, and the worker looks into the lens.
- **Cross-room physical gag**: a locked wide with both characters in frame.
- **Payoff angle**: pick one that contrasts with the setup (over-shoulder → high overhead; third person → first-person POV).
- **Product**:
  - Shoot overhead extreme close-ups and bring the hero object 15–20cm from the lens on the punch.
  - Repeat the same close-up before and after (full sundae → gutted sundae).
- **Reveal wides**: include the context, such as the person filming.
- **Background-plane gags**: keep the subject in focus and the joke character behind them.

## 10. Ending and loop
- Cut a median 0.85s after the last word (p25 0.54).
  - Punch endings: cut 0.05–0.66s after the word.
  - Silent-reaction endings: hold 1.4–1.9s.
- End mid-action with no freeze or fade, on a frame that matches frame 1 (same pose, task and framing), or echo the opening line ('Your total is $8' → 'It's $8').
- Optional: a 1–2s context or product tag after the last word (empty holiday store, finished scoop).

## Mistakes that kill a video
1. Opening on a greeting, an establishing shot or silence.
2. Trimming silence that contains the joke's action, or keeping pauses where nothing happens.
3. Lingering or explaining after the punch, holding more than 1s with no new information.
4. Music under dialogue, or SFX layered over a trending sound.
5. A premise overlay that arrives late, changes, animates or covers faces.
6. Chopping one physical gag into many angles, or cutting away from an escalating payoff.
7. Off-beat cuts in music-led edits.
8. Captions over silent action, or numbers spelled out in words.
