# Food & Drink Edit Playbook

**Baseline (45 viral videos, median with IQR):** length 20.6s (10.9–32.9). Single-item builds run 5–15s and menu montages 20–45s. 11 shots (7–21) · 5.4 cuts/10s (4.0–6.3) · median shot 1.47s (1.23–1.67) · longest shot 3.7s (2.9–4.7) · shortest shot 0.63s (0.53–0.87) · first sound at 0.0s · 7 on-screen words (1–18).

## 1. Hook (0–2s)
- Frame 1 must already show food in motion: a pour, drizzle, smash, scoop, topping drop or sizzle. Sound starts at 0.0s. Never open on an empty counter, a logo or a static plate.
- Strong openers:
  - a macro of the most indulgent action;
  - the 'before' state (empty glass, raw dough, bare waffle) with the first ingredient landing within 1s;
  - the finished dish first, then the build.
- Non-food openers (neon sign, queue, staff skit, meme clip) only work if they pose a question and food lands by 1.2–5s. A 5.9s black/décor intro was the weakest hook in the set.
- Optional title appears by 0.3–1s, 2–6 words, gone by 2.4–4.9s.

## 2. Choosing and framing clips
- Pick moments in this order:
  1. liquid in motion (pours, drizzles, espresso into milk, butter streams);
  2. falling toppings (sugar, seasoning, cheese, nuts);
  3. visible transformations (raw→seared, clear→coloured, plain→loaded);
  4. cut-open cross-sections;
  5. steam and bubbling fat;
  6. the finished hero.
- Prefer macro or close clips where food fills 60–100% of the frame. Shoot at eye level or low (15–45°) with shallow focus. Use overhead only for locked-off flat-lay builds.
- Hands should be gloved (black reads cleanest), or show only tools entering from the top edge. Keep one angle across a layered build so the cuts read as stacking.
- Prefer contrast: bright food on a black griddle or container, a white plate or marble, and specular highlights on sauce. Show drinks in clear glass with the layers visible and the logo turned to camera.
- Order segments by colour change, and save the most saturated colour for the payoff.

## 3. Close-ups and pours
- Pours and drizzles get the longest action holds: 1.5–3.4s, uncut from stream to landing. Never cut mid-fill, mid-layer or mid-melt.
- Show the bare 'canvas' first for 1–3.5s (dry falafel, cream-lined cup, sauce-streaked glass) so the pour reads as the payoff.
- Cover a key pour from two angles (wide, then tight) and cut on the action.
- Sprinkles and topping drops: 0.6–1.5s. In montages over 30s, repeat a pour every 10–15s.

## 4. Money shot
- It is the most indulgent moment: a cheese pull, sauce cascading over melted cheese, an overflow, a cut-open, a glossy drizzle, a lifted dripping spoon.
- Placement: median ~65% of runtime (IQR ~45–81%, range 17–100%).
- Hold: median ~2s (IQR 1.6–3.3; a slow-motion cheese pull ran up to 6s). It should be the longest or second-longest shot, about 2–3× the median shot, with the camera near still.
- Set it up with 3–5 fast shots of 0.5–1.6s (reveal, pour, macro, cut), then cut into it on a strong beat.
- Follow it with one wider finished-hero shot, or end on it. Keep text off it.

## 5. Pacing
- Use two speeds: process shots at 0.5–1.2s each, payoff holds at 2.5–5s. Slow down toward the end.
- Alternate bursts of ~1s shots (tossing, chopping) with one 3–5s near-still hold on steam, melt or a slow pour.
- Menu montages: give each item 1.5–3s, split into a fast 0.6–0.9s detail and a calmer 1.4–2s hold. In ~6s reels, use 0.8–0.95s teasers and give the hero item the last and longest shot.
- Cut on music hits within ~0.1s at reveals and section changes. With speech, cut 0.25–0.35s before each new clause or item name.

## 6. Speed
- Slow to ~50% (if shot at 50–60fps): pours, dust, falling crumbs, cheese pulls, steam, espresso drips.
- Slow motion must stay smooth and silky, never choppy or stuttering. Only slow a clip as far as its frame rate allows: 120–240fps footage can go to 25–50%, 60fps to 50%, and ordinary 30fps phone footage only to a gentle ~85%. Put slow motion on clips shot at high frame rates. If the best pour was shot at 30fps, play it at real speed and hold it longer instead of slowing it.
- Keep at real speed: tossing, chopping, shaking, scooping, hand assembly. Thick pours (smoothie, foam, frappe) already look indulgent at 1x.
- No video used a timelapse. Skip waiting time (baking, melting) with a hard cut to the result or a ~3s cutaway to another station.
- Use speed ramps only as whip transitions, never inside the money shot.

## 7. Text and captions
- Titles: white heavy caps with a shadow or outline, centred upper-middle, typed on word by word on the beats ('NOW INTRODUCING', 'NEW ITEM DROP', 'CITY'S FIRST ___').
- Item names: one per item, switched on that item's first cut. Use a colour-matched label box, or one big numeral for 'choose your' polls.
- Meme-caption alternative: lowercase white sans, 5–20 words, on screen for the whole video, above mid-frame and clear of the food.
- Speech or lyric captions: 1–4 words, white with a black stroke, active word highlighted. Swapping fonts every 0.3–0.6s on the beat adds energy to calm footage.
- No overlay prices (none of the 45 used them). Name and address go on the final food shot only: top third, ≤4 lines, 2.5–5s.
- Keep text off the money shot and the last food hold. Proofread item names.

## 8. Audio
- Default: a trending track or voice clip starting at 0.0s. The best are clips whose words match the action ('they're eating', 'I'm in love, I'm obsessed', 'oh my god', 'more espresso'), cut to one item per phrase.
- Use native sound (scrapes, scoops, tears, bites) only when it is loud and clean. Then duck the music, cut on the action sounds, and cue it with a 'the sound of…' or 'asmr' title.
- A human sound such as claps can serve as the beat; speed it up into the reveal.
- No explicit lyrics.

## 9. Transitions and effects
- Hard cuts by default. Also useful:
  - jump cuts to skip the middle of an action;
  - whip-blur between near-identical setups (same glass, new flavour);
  - a blur-to-sharp rack focus to reveal a hero;
  - a tool sweeping across the lens as a wipe;
  - a white flash to hide a jump.
- Light leaks, prism flares and glitches: 2–4 per video, on section changes, never over texture. Drop them once the pours start.
- Grade: warm and saturated, with boosted yellows/oranges/reds on cooked food, clean whites, deep blacks and mild sharpening.
- These look cheap: oversharpening halos, crunchy steam or metal at 720p, glitch on the finished item, flares across the food.

## 10. Quality
- Export at ≥720x1280 (1080x1920 is better), 30fps, ≥5 Mbps (median 5.3; IQR 4.2–5.7). The 0.7–1.1 Mbps exports smeared sugar, drizzle edges and crumb first.
- Skip soft-focus macros, shaky clips and clips with blown highlights on cream or glaze.

## 11. Ending and loop
- End on food: either a near-still 1–4s hero of the finished item, or mid-motion (the last drizzle, settling cheese).
- Mirror the opening (same framing, raw vs finished) for a seamless loop. Cut 0.2–0.5s after the last word or beat.
- Promos may add name and address on that food shot. A sign or storefront after it should run ≤2s. For takeout, a lid snap of ≤2s works.

## Mistakes that kill it
- No food in the first 2s.
- A static plate held over 1s with no action or camera move.
- Cutting a pour mid-stream, or a hand blocking the drizzle.
- A money shot no longer than the other shots, or flat pacing with no hold.
- Text over the food or the money shot; typos.
- Flat overhead fluorescent light with no highlights; sharp, cluttered backgrounds.
- 4–5s of non-food footage after the last food beat.
- Low-bitrate export, oversharpening, heavy glitch or flare.
