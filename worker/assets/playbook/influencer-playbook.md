# Influencer & Vlog Edit Playbook

Baseline from 33 viral videos:
- **Duration:** median 61s (IQR 36–92s, range 8–221s).
- **Cut rate:** median 2.8 cuts per 10s (IQR 1.1–4.7, range 0–13).
- **Shot length:** median 3.0s (IQR 1.7–6.9s).
- **First spoken word:** 0.0s (latest 1.8s).
- **Frame rate:** 30fps.

## 1. Choose the mode first

Pick one mode from the footage. Everything below depends on it.

- **Voiceover-led** (a VO track over B-roll)
  - 3–7 cuts per 10s, shots 1–3s.
  - VO runs wall to wall at 2.8–5 words/s, with gaps under 0.5s.
  - Runtime 33–82s.
- **Live talk** (usable continuous speech: car reviews, cooking, hangouts, storytime)
  - 0.3–2.8 cuts per 10s, shots 3–8s.
  - Single takes can run 15–30s while banter or a struggle is happening.
  - Runtime 15–220s.
- **Music-only montage** (routine, GRWM, life lately)
  - 4–13 cuts per 10s, median shot 0.3–2.1s, no words.
  - Runtime 8–59s.

## 2. Hook (0–2s)

- Sound starts at 0.0s. Delete everything before the first word or the first beat. Never open on a quiet establishing shot.
- Frame 1 must contain the subject: the payoff held to the lens, the creator mid-sentence, or an action already underway.
- Openers that worked:
  - **Payoff first, then the before.** Show the finished look or dish, then snap to the bare face or raw ingredients at 0.6–2.5s.
  - **Identity plus tension in one sentence.** "22-year-old wannabe influencer working full-time in NYC."
  - **"Come with me to…"** with the goal visible on screen.
  - **A bold claim or brag.** "Who makes the best tabbouleh? Me."
  - **Dropped into the middle of a problem.** "It's not opening."
  - **A spoken three-item agenda.** Shop, food, bike.
- Hook title: 2–7 words. Keep it on screen 1–6s, and remove it at the first cut in fast montages.
- In VO and montage modes, make the first cut by 1–2.5s.

## 3. Formats

**Day in the life**
- Run it chronologically.
- Use the creator's own lines that announce each next stop ("I'm getting hungry") as chapter breaks.
- Add a time label (e.g. 12:58PM) every 8–15s, using only times the creator states or that are visible.
- Let changing daylight act as the progress bar.
- Put the most scenic, high-motion activity in the last third.
- Show the creator's face every 3–5s.
- Use a callback: something bought early reappears in use later.

**GRWM (makeup)**
- One locked framing for the whole video.
- Build each step as a pair: product held to the lens (0.5–0.7s), then the application (0.6–0.9s).
- Order: skin → base → sculpt → colour → eyes → lips → final pose.
- Hold longest (2–2.6s) on the steps with the biggest visible change and on the final pose.

**GRWM (grooming)**
- Mark each chapter with one wide reveal (cabinet, closet).
- Give the most satisfying sequence 6–7s across 3–4 clips.
- End on what they got ready for (friends, an event).

**Routine**
- Open on an entrance (door opening, arriving home).
- Reuse one locked anchor angle 3 or more times, each time in a new outfit.
- Mark the turn with an outfit-change cut.
- Use same-frame jump cuts for switch-on payoffs (lights off → on).
- End at the destination (gym, suitcase).

**Family, kids and pets**
- Use kids, partners or pets in real, unscripted actions (grabbing a juice box, coloring, a cat wandering in).
- Space them every 10–15s, 1.5–3s each.
- Keep off-camera family lines.

**Taste test or review**
- Give every item the same arc: shown at the lens → named → bite → 3–6s silent reaction → verdict.
- Segments run 9–17s.
- Order from good to best. Keep the creator's line pre-announcing the favourite.
- Keep honest negative verdicts; they make the positive ones believable.

**Haul, unboxing or gift**
- Open on the product closed. Put the main reveal at about 25% of the runtime.
- Give each component a 2–5s mini-reveal.
- Hold readable details (labels, engravings) still for 2–3s.
- Place the CTA at 65–80% of the runtime.
- End on a small personal add-on.

**Recipe or come-shop-with-me**
- Show the finished result at 0–3s, then flash back to the start.
- Shopping shots: 0.9–1.4s per item.
- Assembly from one locked top-down angle: 1–2s per step.
- The final shot is the taste reaction, held the longest.

**Storytime or hangout (raw)**
- Keep long takes with dead air when the struggle is the point.
- Cut right after key lines.
- Keep any end game the footage contains (guessing the bill), set up about 20s before the reveal.

**Life lately montage**
- Clips of 0.25–0.35s, cut on every 1–2 beats. Total under 9s.
- Show the creator (faceless is fine) every 2–3s.
- Repeat the strongest creator shot at the midpoint.
- Use one small label that stays on screen the whole time.

## 4. Story

- Open a question in the hook and answer it late. "Which bag?" was answered at 77% of the runtime.
- Place a mini payoff at 35–45% of the runtime, such as a before/after.
- Place a dilemma ("torn between A and B") around 70%, and reveal the choice about 6s later.
- After the main reveal, a second small problem or a bonus item carries the last 15–20s.
- Twists that land in the final words work well.
- Calling back the opening line at the end closes the loop.
- Honest anticlimaxes are fine ("didn't finish one video, calling it a night").

## 5. Personality

Keep these moments:
- Silent reaction faces of 1.5–6s (eyes closed while chewing, eye-rolls, sideways stares).
- Hand over mouth.
- Objects pushed toward the lens.
- Talking with a mouth full.
- Stumbles and self-corrections.
- Confessions followed by gratitude.
- Off-camera people pushing back.

Other rules:
- After a verdict, add one 0.5–1s punch-in on the silent partner's face.
- Even voiceover-only videos need 2–3 face moments: hook, middle, final reaction.

## 6. Picking and ordering moments

- Prefer moments with visible change, a reaction, or satisfying hand action.
- Trim each action to the instant it completes.
- Shot lengths:
  - Voiceover mode: 1.5–3s.
  - Montage: 0.5–2s.
  - Live talk: 3–8s.
  - The payoff shot (first sip, final look) always gets the longest hold in the video.
- Order:
  - Chronological for days and routines.
  - Grouped by category for lists (main, sides, dessert, drinks).
  - Escalating within each group.
- Never show the same activity twice in a row.

## 7. Voice and text

**Voiceover mode**
- Narrate exactly what is on screen.
- Cut to each item within 0.1–0.5s of its noun being spoken.
- Spoken chapter markers keep it moving ("first things first", "next up", "last but not least").

**Live talk mode**
- Trim pauses longer than 1–1.5s.
- Start each shot on the first word of a new bit.
- In advice clips, use one sentence per shot, cut within 0.05s of the sentence start.

**Music-only mode**
- The title plus the visible progression carry the story.
- Where lyric words match visuals, put them together (outfit on "dress").

**Text as inner voice**
- For no-VO footage, text can act as the creator's inner voice: at most 3 lines per 100s, 5–9 words each, held for the one clip it comments on.
- Text, prices, scores and place names must come only from the creator's speech or what is visible. Never invent them.

## 8. Comments, shares, saves and follows

- **Comments**
  - Unresolved debates and scores (6/10 versus 9.5/10).
  - A number viewers can guess.
  - Products left unnamed in montages ("what's that?").
  - Quotable opinions.
- **Shares and tags:** lines that point at a person ("what girls wish guys smelled like", "tag the friend who…").
- **Saves:** 📍 location pins, spoken prices and tips.
- **Follows:** the creator's own series framing ("Day 3").
- **Explicit asks that work:**
  - A spoken closing question over the opening composition.
  - "Comment this emoji if you stayed to the end."

## 9. Pacing

- In voiceover mode, put the fastest cuts in the first 6s, slow to 2–3s for process shots, and tighten to 2–3s again in the last quarter.
- In montages, snap every cut within about 0.05s of a beat hit.
- In live talk, energy comes from speech density (2.5–3.7 words/s), not from cuts.
- Mix low-motion tripod shots with high-motion handheld shots (runs, walks) around the middle third to lift energy.

## 10. Speed

- None of the 33 videos used slow motion, speed ramps or timelapses.
- Keep everything at normal speed and compress time by trimming and jump cuts within the same framing.
- Speed effects break beat sync and make the footage feel less real.

## 11. On-screen text and captions

**Voiceover mode captions**
- Bold white uppercase with a dark shadow, 1–4 words at a time.
- Highlight the active word in a pink or red rounded box.
- Place 70–78% down the frame. Keep all text out of the bottom 20%.
- Make the hook title a different style (a coloured box), and proofread brand names.

**Live talk captions**
- Usually none.
- Add them only for dialect, weak audio or distant voices. Use full sentences, white serif or clean sans, placed lower-middle.

**Aesthetic day-in-the-life captions**
- Tiny lowercase phrase captions of 2–5 words, or a typewriter style.

**Labels**
- Small, lowercase, 2–7 words, no box, centred at mid-frame or upper third.
- Kinds: 📍 venue name, times, item name, then price swapped to score after the verdict.
- Sponsor disclosure goes small at the top left.

## 12. Audio

- **Montage:** one trending track, with cuts on its hits.
- **Voiceover:** a clean VO over a quiet music bed.
- **Live or raw footage:**
  - No music; the voice is the soundtrack.
  - Keep ASMR sounds loud (crunch, crinkle, pour, chopping).
  - Cut so the loudest bite lands in the shot.
- Never bury speech under a song.

## 13. Transitions and effects

- All 33 videos used hard cuts only. These cut devices feel authentic:
  - Match on action.
  - A location jump hidden inside motion blur.
  - A same-frame jump cut.
  - An outfit-change cut.
  - The recurring anchor angle.
- For close-ups, use the creator pushing the object toward the lens, not digital zoom.
- Overproduced: designed whips, flashes, spins, heavy filters, stickers.

## 14. Quality

- **Warm and real:** window or practical light; hard sun for sparkle and texture; tripod for routines and talk; handheld for POV and walks.
- **Messy:** swooping past readable details; the shakiest handheld seconds (trim them); duplicated chunks.
- Lo-fi footage raises trust in reviews and storytimes.

## 15. Ending and loop

- Cut 0.1–0.7s after the last word or final beat hit. Never leave a tail.
- End on the final reaction, the reveal, or a final frame that matches the first, so the video loops.
- No outro under 20s.
- Sponsored sign-offs can hold pretty B-roll for 1.5–2s.

## Common flop causes

- A silent or greeting intro before the first word.
- Answering the hook question early.
- Cutting away before the silent reaction or the verdict.
- Removing stumbles, kids or off-camera voices to look "clean".
- Wrap-ups padded to 45s, or audio and video chunks repeated by mistake.
- Music drowning out speech or natural sound.
- Stylized transitions, speed effects or heavy filters.
- The same angle or activity twice in a row.
- Text that invents facts, prices or claims.
