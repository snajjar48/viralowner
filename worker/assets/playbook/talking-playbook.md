# Talking-Head Edit Playbook
*Built from 55 viral talking-head videos. Figures are medians, with p25–p75 in brackets. The dense end of the range (≥4.2 words/s, ≥3.5 cuts/10s) suits tips and lists. Median pace suits stories.*

| Target | Value |
|---|---|
| First word | 0.00s (max 0.18s) |
| Speech share | 99% (98–100%) |
| Median word gap | 0.00s |
| Gaps over 0.3s | 2 (0–4); longest 0.38s (0.24–0.52) |
| Fillers | 0 in every video |
| Pace | 3.8 words/s (3.3–4.2) |
| Cuts | 2.3 per 10s (1.1–3.5); median shot 3.6s (2.3–6.3) |
| Length | 44s (18–72) |
| Tail after last word | 0.28s (0.17–0.41) |

## 1. Hook (0–5s)
- The first word lands at 0.00s. Cut all setup frames. Clipping a leading “if” or “so” is fine if the caption shows it. Never open with “hi guys, today I want to…”.
- The hook sentence runs faster than the body: 4.5–5.5 words/s versus about 3.8. The full promise lands in 2–4s.
- The first sentence needs at least one of these:
  - a number (“10 hooks”)
  - a contrarian command (“Stop posting more”)
  - a specific result (“0 to 100K in a month”)
  - an audience call-out (“as a creator”)
  - a challenge (“You’re not funny”)
  If it has none, suggest a rewrite.
- Show proof by about 3s. When the hook claims a result, cut in the screenshot (view-count grid, profile card, dashboard) on the number word. In these videos it appeared at 0.56–2.9s.
- Stack a second hook within 1–4s. Use an escalation (“and you can actually change it”), a proof line, or an end-reward clause (“but they get increasingly more niche”).
- Put a title on screen from frame 0:
  - 2 lines in the top 12–30% of the frame, restating the promise with its number.
  - Optional smaller qualifier line, e.g. “(niche edition)”.
  - Style: bold white with a shadow, or heavy caps with one yellow or lime word. Use all-lowercase for confessional tones.
  - Remove it at the first cut (3–6s) in stories and explainers. Keep it for the whole video in lists.
- For lists, show the full structure at frame 0: empty numbered slots, one thumbnail per item, masked table rows or an unticked checklist. It works as a progress bar.

## 2. Jump cuts
- Trim breaths between sentences to 0.1–0.25s. No other gap stays over 0.3s.
- Keep only 1–3 beats of 0.35–0.8s: before a twist, right after the key number (“$300,000”), or before a demo.
- Cut on the first syllable of the next sentence (±0.1s). Cut mid-sentence only to bring an overlay or b-roll in on its keyword, or to hide a take splice under continuous captions.
- Cut rate by format:
  - Lists with one take per item: 4–7 cuts per 10s (shots 0.7–2.3s).
  - Tips and explainers: 2–4 per 10s.
  - Stories and rants: 1–2 per 10s.
  - A zero-cut video only works at 3.5+ words/s with no gaps and a text change at least every 1.6s.
- Keep a stumble only if it proves the point (“leave the bloopers in”).

## 3. Punch-ins and visual rhythm
- Most videos used no animated zoom. With a single framing, alternate 1.0x and 1.1–1.2x across cuts:
  - Tight on thesis, “here’s why” and benefit lines; hold 1–2.5s.
  - Wide for lists and big gestures.
  - Tightest frame (1.3–1.4x) on the final line.
  - Optional push-in of about 1.3x during the hook.
- Keep the eyes at the same height (about 38–40% from the top) across framings.
- Something must change every 1–3s: a cut, overlay, caption style, emoji or graphic step. The bare face never holds past about 7s. Long takes only work with an animated speaker and overlays mid-shot.
- If the user filmed several angles or locations, alternate them every 6–30s and return to the tightest one for the payoff.
- Front-load density. Change every 1–4s in the first 30s, allow 6–11s holds once the argument flows, then speed up again for the takeaway.

## 4. Captions
Use one system per video:
- **Punchy tips:** 1–3 words, heavy sans, ALL CAPS, white with a dark stroke, spoken word turns yellow. Place at 72–80% of frame height.
- **Calm or aesthetic:** one word at a time, lowercase, white rounded sans, soft shadow, no box. Place at about 33% (under a top image) or 60–70%.
- **Stories and rants:** small native-style white phrases of 1–5 words, about 4% of frame height, no highlights. Place at 55–75%.

Rules for all systems:
- Emphasize about one word every 2–4s with yellow or lime caps, italic serif or a pill box. If you color-code, use red for the problem, green for the action, yellow for the topic.
- Never overlap graphics. Move captions between about 30–35% and 65–78% as overlays come and go.
- Write numbers as numerals. Censor swears or swap them for an emoji. Fix misspoken numbers with an asterisk correction. Proofread auto-captions.
- Drop speech captions while a list, table or label already shows the words. Two lines maximum; never frame-wide.

## 5. On-screen text
- Section headers take one of two forms:
  - “#1” plus a label at top-left (about 14–17% height), appearing on “Number one” and held for the whole section.
  - A 1–2 word yellow caps keyword with a black outline, mid-frame, 0.5–1s after “Step N”.
- List labels are 2–4 words, appear within 0.1s of the spoken keyword, and stay on screen as the list builds. Keep template blanks as [X]/[Y].
- After about 8 items, flip to a fresh page or layout (seen at 25–60% of runtime).
- Animation:
  - Hard pops for most text.
  - A typewriter reveal (about 0.8s) for titles.
  - Stacked kinetic words (small connector words, big accent keyword) only on the hook and key lines.

## 6. Graphics, overlays, transitions
- Every number or claim gets proof on its word: a screenshot, a counter that lands on the spoken figure, or a red hand-drawn circle on the number.
- When an app or tool is named, cut to a screen recording within about 0.5s. Show it as a large picture-in-picture (60–90% width) or a phone mockup over a blurred speaker. Add a tap hand, highlight box or about 2x punch-in on the exact button.
- For frameworks, build the diagram one element per spoken item and circle each as it is named. Show a full recap at about 85–90%.
- When replying to a comment, keep the comment card in the top 20–28% until the pivot line.
- Layout: graphics in the top 10–45%, face in the middle, captions low. Nothing important in the bottom 12% or the lower-right.
- Use hard cuts. Allow at most one stylised transition (light-leak flash, hand over lens), at the hook-to-body or explanation-to-demo seam.

## 7. Pattern interrupts
- 1–3s of literal b-roll for each concrete noun or example.
- A 0.6–2s meme when a line matches one.
- An emoji beside punchy captions every 1–3s.
- Demonstrate tips on the speaker’s own footage: zoom on “zoom”, pop text on “pop up”, dark grade on “bad lighting”.
- Switch format around 50–60% of runtime (screen demo, new location).
- Clear all overlays for section resets and for the CTA.
- Sound effects: pop or click on text and checkmarks, whoosh on overlays and transitions, riser into reveals, ding on the CTA.

## 8. Audio
- A close, clean voice dominates. Most videos had no music bed; any music sits clearly below the voice. No silence except the planned beats.

## 9. Length, structure, ending
- Length by format: mantra 7–10s, rapid list 12–20s, tips or tutorial 25–60s, story or framework 55–120s.
- **Lists:** order items familiar to novel, or tactical to emotional. Put the best item at 82–95%.
- **“One thing” videos:** 2–3 “it’s not X” denials, then the reveal at 20–25%.
- **Tutorials:** demo at 50–60%, a “screenshot this” card at 80–90%.
- **Stories and challenges:** stakes and a number in the first 5s, time-stamp chapters every 6–10s, the result at 75–80%, then an honest reflection.
- Cut 0.15–0.4s after the last word. No outro.
- Endings:
  - A loop: the last line could open the video, or the final frame matches the first.
  - A comment-keyword CTA shown in caps and quotes in the last 5–10%, optionally also at about 30% right after the proof.
  - A series CTA (“part 2”, “day four”).
  - A follow CTA framed as the last list item.
  - “Save this” for utility videos.

## 10. Mistakes that kill it
1. A dead first half-second, or a greeting.
2. An untrimmed tail. One video ran 95s of black and static footage after the content ended.
3. Pauses over 0.4s that aren’t a planned beat.
4. A bare face for more than 7s with nothing changing.
5. A claim with no on-screen proof.
6. Frame-wide captions, two text systems competing, or text in the UI zone.
7. A hook that doesn’t say who it’s for.
8. Black, upside-down or 3:4 footage left in.
9. Auto-caption typos left uncorrected.
