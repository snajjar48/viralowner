# Research notes: influencer and vlog short-form (outside sources, secondary to the videos)

Most of these are creator guides and marketing blogs, not platform data; treat numbers as rough guides.

## Hooks and retention
- Viewers decide in roughly 1.5-2 seconds; the opening needs a visual and an audible hook together (short-form has no thumbnail). Open with movement, on-screen text or a clear promise, never a slow intro.
- Common shape: 0-3s hook, ~3-10s context (why this matters / what's happening), then the moments, then a payoff.
- Completion and rewatches are the strongest signals; a looping ending multiplies them. Shorter videos complete far more often (reported ~84% for 16-30s vs ~68% for 31-60s; single source, rough).
- A visual change every ~2-3 seconds is commonly recommended, but purposeful, not chaotic.

## Day in the life / daily vlogs
- Most run 60 seconds or less. Show the highlights of the day, not all of it; cut any moment that doesn't earn its place.
- Beat order that works: hook, setup, 3-5 middle moments, a closing shot.
- Routines and day-in-the-life play best in chronological order (unlike travel recaps, which want fast variety).
- Two styles: a voiceover explaining each moment, or aesthetic snippets over a song with text.
- Short aesthetic moments (morning coffee, a walk, lunch prep, skincare) are where viewers linger.
- Rhythm matters more than production value. A consistent look (same filter, font, sound style) makes a creator recognisable.

## Get ready with me (GRWM) and storytime
- The routine is the backdrop; the story or the talk to camera is what keeps people (a "casual call with a close friend before heading out"). Storytime GRWM (funny, dramatic or personal stories while getting ready) is the most viral variant; mixing routine + story raises watch time.
- Intimacy and unpolished moments (pajamas, no makeup, real life) make the creator feel like a friend; very curated content feels out of reach.
- ASMR-like routine sounds (caps clicking, brushes) are part of the appeal: don't bury them under music when they're clean.
- A visible before → after is a natural payoff to end on.
- Product mentions must stay subtle; an obvious push breaks the spell. Organic GRWM reportedly earns far higher engagement than paid campaigns (one vendor's data).

## Family and mom/dad vlogs
- Everyday parenting moments are the material; viral ones turn an ordinary moment into a relatable punchline or sweet payoff.
- Real struggles and honest moments build loyal viewers more than highlight reels.
- Trends work when adapted to the family (kids as the twist).
